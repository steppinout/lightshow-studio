# Sequence audit notes

The early product audit reviewed 9 residential-style sequencing packages and aggregated their effect usage patterns.

## Aggregate findings

- 9 sequences analyzed
- 43,857 effects observed
- 45 unique effect names identified
- top effect families are dominated by straightforward state, color, and accent patterns

## Top effect names

The most common effect names in the audit were:

- On
- Shockwave
- Off
- Color Wash
- SingleStrand
- Lightning
- Ripple
- Pinwheel
- Curtain
- Faces

## Product insight

The most important takeaway is that simple, practical effects dominate real home-grown sequences.

This means the app must make basic sequencing extremely fast and intuitive. Users do not primarily need a large catalog of exotic effects on day one; they need quick access to the most common visual building blocks:

- on/off states
- color washes
- pulse and accent patterns
- motion patterns with clear energy
- simple group-level effects that feel visually pleasing without deep tuning

## Design implication

The product should center its first experience around a simple, high-speed workflow for:

- turning props on and off
- applying color states
- adding accent motion
- layering a few effect ideas over a song structure
- previewing the result in context

The app should not make the user parse a complex library of low-level effect definitions before they can make a satisfying show.
